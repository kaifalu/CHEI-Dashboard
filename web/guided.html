START HERE

From your question to a useful result

Explore one indicator or combine several in the same place. No GIS experience or software installation is required. Each step opens only the choices you need now.

1 · LOCATION

Select a place

Choose the whole county, a five-digit ZIP code, Commissioner Precinct 1–4, or an 11-digit census-tract GEOID. ZIP 77007 is one example. Your chosen place stays in focus while you explore the results.

2 · PRECIPITATION

Choose a rainfall view

Select Rainfall at one warming level and choose +1.5, +2.0, +2.5, or +3.0°C, or select the supplied Change between two warming levels. Use Continue without a precipitation input for a community-only combination or a supplied index.

3 · INDICATORS

Check what you want to combine

Combine indicators offers 17 families across five groups. Start with the main indicators, open a group, search by name, or use Show all indicators. Add CHEI, hotspots, housing stock, density, land use, and other inputs. Choose at least two; the dashboard explains when two representations would count the same source measure twice. Use Explore one layer to see a layer in its original display geography.

4 · PERIODS & RULES

Set periods and define a match

Choose periods for population, households, employment, CHEI, and land use, and a housing type for grid or housing-location summaries. For numeric inputs, define “high” with a countywide percentile. For land use and fixed hotspots, check the named categories you want. Then choose whether ALL, ANY, or AT LEAST a chosen number of conditions must be met.

5 · RESULTS

Read the overlap and its inputs

The map zooms to your place. In Combined overlap, darker fill means more selected conditions are met; a gold outline identifies tracts meeting your rule. Use the individual indicator buttons above the map to inspect one input while keeping the whole combination selected.

CHECK THE EVIDENCE

Click a tract to see all its selected values. Review the table below the charts, choose All in the selected place if you want to see nonmatches, or select Download screening CSV. Print screening brief opens a compact report of the current location and rule.

Try four indicators together

A practical starting path

Select ZIP 77007 → Rainfall at one warming level → +2.5°C → Combine indicators. Click Select the four main indicators to include precipitation, SVI, housing-unit growth, and population. In Step 4 choose 2050 population, keep the 80th percentile, and choose ALL selected conditions are met. Then select Show my results.

Ask a broader question

Read how many tracts meet all four conditions. If none do, the combination may simply be restrictive. Return to Step 4 and choose AT LEAST 3 of 4 to ask where several conditions coincide. Compare the inputs and the changed results. These are demonstration settings, not a recommended threshold for investment or policy.

Understand what the map is showing

A count and a matching rule answer different questions

3 of 4 conditions met means three selected numeric cutoffs or category choices are satisfied in that tract. It matches an AT LEAST 3 rule, but does not match an ALL 4 rule. Changing the rule changes the matching tracts; changing the percentile can also change which individual values count as high.

Each input has its own units and period

SVI retains its 2020 reference. Housing-unit growth retains its projected 2020–2050 change from supplied changing-parcel data; it is different from household growth. A precipitation input retains its warming condition. Population, households, and employment use the period you selected for each.

Questions and troubleshooting

What does “high” mean?

Each numeric indicator is compared with its own countywide percentile cutoff, shown in its own units. Category indicators use the named choices you check in Step 4 instead. At the 80th percentile, high generally means the upper fifth of valid county values. Values equal to the cutoff count as high, so ties can include more tracts. Calculations use unrounded values; the interface rounds for readability. The same county cutoffs apply in every ZIP or precinct. A high population or more growth is not automatically a harmful condition, and this count is not a new CHEI or flood probability.

Why is there no single year for my combination?

The source indicators have different references. The checklist combines their spatial patterns without creating new projections or treating them as a synchronized future scenario. There is no supplied future SVI, and a warming level is not assigned a calendar year. Step 4 shows the reference of every checked factor.

What if a tract has missing values?

A tract must have data for every checked indicator to enter the combined screen. Incomplete tracts are counted separately and excluded from ALL, ANY, and AT LEAST rules. The reported matching percentage uses complete-data tracts as its denominator. Individual input cards use that indicator's own available values, so their denominators can differ. Missing data are not treated as zero or low values.

What does it mean when no tract matches?

It means no evaluated tract meets that particular rule and set of cutoffs; it does not mean no climate exposure or vulnerability exists. Inspect individual input maps and choose All in the selected place in the table to see the underlying values. Change a rule or percentile only if the resulting question is useful. If no tract has complete selected data, a matching percentage cannot be calculated.

Which layers can I combine?

The 17 indicator families represent all 26 original analytical layer keys through period, housing-type, category, and geographic-summary choices. Combinations use census tracts. Model points become within-tract means, housing-grid cells become intersecting-cell medians, housing locations use complete tract record counts, and land use uses the dominant named category among changing parcels. Original maps remain in Explore one layer. A combination needs at least two distinct measures. Exact duplicate source fields are rejected; related indicators remain selectable with an explanation.

Why do some combinations have little data?

Model-point summaries have values in 197 of 1,115 tracts, housing-grid summaries in 1,109, and changed-parcel land-use summaries in 787. No nearby value is substituted for a missing point or parcel summary. Combining many inputs can reduce the number of complete-data tracts. Step 4 shows each input’s coverage before you run the screen.

How do land-use and hotspot choices work?

Check one or more named categories in Step 4. For example, selecting projected Residential land use means a tract’s dominant category among the supplied changing parcels must be Residential. It does not mean the entire tract is residential. A hotspot choice matches its original fixed category. Categories are never treated as numeric high-to-low scores.

How do individual indicator buttons affect my results?

Buttons above the map switch between Combined overlap and a view of whether one checked input meets its numeric or category condition. They do not remove indicators, change the matching rule, or alter the summary, table export, or brief. The gold match outlines continue to represent the complete combination. To change the calculation, edit the selections in the guided steps.

What do ZIP and precinct summaries mean?

They use whole source features that intersect the selected boundary, including boundary touches. Tract medians give each tract equal weight. These are not ZIP/precinct totals, population-weighted averages, or official area indices. A tract may extend beyond your selected boundary or appear in more than one area's screen.

What happens when I click the legend or a tract?

A legend class highlights matching features within your selected place; use the legend's zoom or clear controls to inspect and reset it. A tract click opens its values without replacing the selected ZIP or precinct. Choose Use this tract as my location only when you intend to change the focus. A map highlight does not change the geographic scope of the summary or exported results.

Does the precipitation choice change CHEI or fixed hotspots?

No. In both modes, supplied CHEI retains its original climate assumptions. Fixed compound hotspots always use +2.5°C precipitation, 2020–2050 household growth, and 2020 SVI with the source thresholds. Their explanation identifies the actual inputs. Use Combine indicators for your own precipitation condition, factors, and cutoffs; it remains a separate exploratory classification.

What is included in the CSV?

Download screening CSV includes every tract in the selected geographic screen, even when the on-screen table is showing only matches. It records exact factor values, units, reference periods, numeric cutoffs or selected category names, condition flags, completeness, and whether the rule is met. Preserve the location, settings, and source limitations when reusing it. The exported screen is not a new authoritative dataset.

How do I print or save the brief?

Select Print screening brief, allow the new report tab, and choose Print / Save as PDF. Use Letter or A4, default scaling, and turn off browser headers/footers; check the print preview before saving. The brief describes the full selected place and combination regardless of the input tab or highlighted legend class. Its simplified boundary is an orientation map, not a separate thematic risk map. A brief with many indicators can span several pages.

Why is Next unavailable, or an option missing?

Complete the current selection: ZIP codes require five digits, tract GEOIDs require eleven, and a combination requires at least two indicators. Precipitation is unavailable in the checklist until a rainfall choice is made in Step 2. Individual-layer periods and representations appear only when supported by the supplied data. Two choices that use the identical underlying measure cannot be counted twice. Category indicators require at least one category choice in Step 4.

Can I work offline, and why might the basemap be blank?

The HTML embeds the analytical data, application code, maps, charts, and export/report functions. It works locally without Python. The default background uses embedded boundaries and makes no tile requests, so no API key is needed. On an HTTP/HTTPS-hosted page, Map display options → Background map optionally adds OpenStreetMap streets. Street tiles need internet access; switch back to Embedded boundaries if they are unavailable. No online street tiles are bundled for offline use. Static single-family housing points are sampled and the optional server also caps displayed points; use the full-count grid or supplied tract housing layers for other housing summaries.

Potential applications

Use combinations to prepare community adaptation discussions, explore housing and infrastructure needs, compare growth patterns, or teach climate and social-data interpretation. Results identify where to investigate further. Add local drainage, infrastructure, housing, and community evidence before making a specific decision.

For the earlier all-controls interface and Quick Decision shortcuts, use the Advanced explorer included in the ZIP package. The guided HTML does not require that extra file.
